A house, alone,
in fog.

Eight boards for the Space homepage: how the house looks at each hour, how the fog carries the scene changes, what the screen chrome is allowed to be, and the material and fog palette the build will follow.

BOARDS GENERATED WITH OPENAI IMAGES FROM THE HOUSE PHOTOGRAPHS · REFERENCE: IGLOO.INC · NOT SURVEYED GEOMETRY

The direction in five rules

One object

The house is the only thing on screen. No neighbours, no traffic, no walkers, no furniture beyond what a scene needs. The street is a plane that fades into fog.

Fog carries the change

Every scene change happens inside fog at its thickest. Between scenes there is an empty beat, like board 02, where almost nothing is visible. The viewer never sees the swap.

One floor at a time

Each floor is shown as an open-top diorama in its own light: café morning on the teak floor, the courtyard at four, the terrace at sundown. Board 04 is the quality bar.

Light is the clock

The camera on the house stays fixed for the exterior. Only the light and the fog colour change from dawn pink to night blue. The six fog chips below are the day.

Chrome in the corners

Four small monospace labels only: wordmark top-left, Book a call top-right, Scroll bottom-left, clock bottom-right. Nothing is ever pinned to the house. No headlines over the centre.

What the boards are not

They are not surveyed geometry. The house model comes from the as-built drawing. Boards 01, 06 and 07 are the reference for the front elevation, light and framing. Where a board and the drawing disagree on a dimension, the drawing wins.

Dawn exterior

Grounded in p19 for the street façade and p05 for the gate. Keep the stepped roofline, restrained blossoms, solitary framing and generous fog margins. Reject the first attempt's dense foreground canopy, extra planting and sun flare; these were removed. Treat the cleaned-up façade and symmetric tree placement as art direction, not as surveyed geometry. The real address is 1172; visible numerals were omitted to honour the no-text image requirement.

Fog transition

The exact dawn composition dissolves into warm pale fog, leaving only a faint upper stepped roof silhouette.

Grounded indirectly in p19 and p05 through board 01. Keep the empty beat and continuity of roof position; reject readable rooms, foreground objects, ghost labels and dramatic cloud effects. This frame deliberately has higher opacity than the settled scenes so the next floor can appear unseen.

03

Café morning

An open-top ground-floor living-room section holds two small café tables on polished teak, with brass lanterns and morning light through glazed teak doors.

Grounded in p25; the lawn relationship is supported by the supplied Living 1 context and photo labels for p27 and p21. Keep the floor reflections, sparse furniture, recognizable glazing and empty fog beyond. Reject additional café equipment, crowds and the initial closed ceiling; the final section removes it. The supporting beam, table settings and section boundary are invented presentation devices, and the lawn is mostly concealed by fog.

04

Courtyard afternoon

A downward view into the first-floor double-height court at 4 pm shows an olive tree in a concrete planter, sandstone flagstones, three woven nest pendants, teak louvred doors and one group of three.

Grounded directly in p11 and p01. Keep the compact court, tree-to-door relationship, varied pendant heights and soft light through the skylight frame. Reject an enlarged plaza, a second tree or confusion with the separate basement court. Seating, people and exposed section edges are conceptual additions; validate final roof/skylight construction against the house.

05

Terrace sundown

The second-floor terrace at 6:30 pm has terracotta paving, a timber bench, white parapet and four people talking beneath one sparse strand of warm lights, against an orange-to-violet atmosphere.

Grounded in p33. Keep the long terrace proportion, bench, parapet openings, four-person scale and fog around its base. Reject neighbors, drying racks, party furnishings and the initial sun disc; the final framing pulls back and removes the sun. String lights and occupants are proposed use, not photo evidence. The side-room roof is still prominent and touches the left image edge; reduce that mass or widen the camera further in the eventual 3D sequence.

06

Night club

At 10 pm the same closed house and street camera return, every visible window glowing warm amber, with delicate roof string lights against dark blue fog and a faint street plane.

Grounded in p19 and p05 through the dawn anchor. Keep matching window placement, contained warmth and low street contrast. Reject neon, club signs, people, cars or another building. Window glow and roof lights are an imagined nighttime treatment; their physical installation and real light spill remain to be checked.

07

HUD and type

The dawn hero carries only four small monospace corner labels: SPACE, Book a call, Scroll and 06:00 IST.

Grounded in the same p19/p05 exterior, with chrome restraint informed by the supplied Igloo frames and README. Keep the empty centre and clear gap between chrome and architecture. Reject central headlines, panels, survey lines and labels pinned to the house. SPACE is a provisional typeset wordmark, and the clock is illustrative static text. An image edit can introduce small pixel differences from board 01; use the original hero as the actual compositing background during implementation.

Palette and materials

A flat board pairs lime-white plaster, teak, terrazzo, sandstone, terracotta, red oxide, charcoal steel and foliage with six fog chips.

Grounded in p19/p05 for plaster and gate, p25 and p11/p01 for teak and sandstone, p16 for terrazzo, p15 for red oxide, p08 for steel, p33 for terracotta and p24 for entrance foliage/green bench context. Keep the muted texture range and quiet layout; reject treating these hex values as measured physical material colours. Material values are proposed approximations.
