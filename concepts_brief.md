# Fourthspace: Founding Sponsorship Model and Brand Direction

* **Author:** HSR Founders Club / Fourthspace working team
* **Date:** 2026-10-26
* **Status:** Working concept after six-lens Goldfish review; not approved for external circulation

## Overview

Fourthspace is a working name for a proposed café and event space in HSR Layout, Bengaluru, with a long-term ambition to become an international network of **professional-opportunity houses**. The internal analogy is “LinkedIn as a house.” The public proposition is a place where founders and people around startups can meet, exchange ideas, attend programs, and discover professional opportunities through repeated, trusted encounters. This is a deliberate difference from The Commons’ meaning-making category and continues the HSR Founders Club story.

The concept grew out of HSR Founders Club. Its founding inspiration is the katte, a traditional neighbourhood gathering place in Karnataka. Katte should inform hospitality, informal access, repeated encounters, and low-pressure participation; it should not become decorative naming or typography alone.

The immediate deliverable is a standalone sponsorship prospectus that a brand decision-maker can understand, evaluate, and forward internally. The business now has two stated recurring revenue engines: founder membership and brand sponsorship. Founder membership is restricted to founders and costs ₹2 lakh for the first membership year, then ₹1 lakh annually thereafter. The proposed sponsorship model is up to 20 category-exclusive annual partners paying ₹15 lakh (₹1.5 million) each. At full sell-through, sponsorship equals ₹3 crore (₹30 million) in gross annual contracted value before taxes, discounts, sales costs, activation costs, and operating costs. It is not booked revenue, a demand forecast, or proof that the build is funded.

The founding-community figures supplied for this brief are:

- 120+ HSR Founders Club events.
- Approximately 5,200 event attendees; the definition and unique-person count still need confirmation.
- 3,000+ founders in the HSR-area directory.
- 7,000 newsletter signups; this does not establish current engagement or sponsor-contact consent.

These figures belong to HSR Founders Club. They establish a relevant operating history, not Fourthspace footfall, unique reach, leads, conversions, or renewal value.

## Analysis

### 1. The current package is easier to describe than to buy

Menu placement, merchandise, QR codes, events, and demos are widely available forms of marketing inventory. They do not, by themselves, justify ₹15 lakh. The differentiated hypothesis is narrower: repeated, relevant, in-person engagement with a specific Bengaluru founder community, delivered through useful experiences rather than advertising volume.

A sponsor will compare this with digital acquisition, newsletter sponsorship, established event sponsorship, creator campaigns, and hosting its own event. The prospectus therefore needs to explain which sponsor objective the house serves: product discovery, customer research, voluntary conversations, community association, or another agreed outcome. It must not promise superior ROI before evidence exists.

**Counterargument:** delaying the annual offer for pilots could slow fundraising and weaken the urgency of founding-partner sales. The alternative is to test the activation through existing HSR Founders Club events while developing conditional annual pre-sales. In either case, unsigned interest cannot finance construction as though it were collected cash.

### 2. Historical community evidence needs strict boundaries

Every figure needs a reporting period, source, definition, and owner. In particular:

- Attendance must distinguish registrations, check-ins, cumulative attendee-visits, and unique people.
- Directory entries must not be described as active members or reachable sponsor leads.
- Newsletter signups must not be described as active readers without engagement evidence.
- The four figures must never be added into one audience number.
- HSR Founders Club records do not imply consent for Fourthspace marketing or sponsor introductions.

The strongest additional evidence would be dated examples of representative events, audience roles and company stages where known, repeat participation, and documented sponsor or community interest. Do not invent demographic, income, seniority, purchase-intent, footfall, or conversion claims.

### 3. The annual offer needs a bounded core, not an unlimited benefit menu

The proposed annual product should be standardized enough to price and deliver. A candidate structure for validation is:

**Core annual partnership**

- Category exclusivity at the HSR house for the contracted term, using a written category definition.
- Templated recognition in a shared sponsor system, such as a menu or partner display.
- A fixed allocation of higher-attention rotating feature slots and approved QR offers.
- One sponsor-led event-space use per calendar quarter, subject to capacity and booking rules.
- Standard quarterly reporting using metrics the house actually collects.

**Separately scoped or priced**

- Co-created menu products and custom physical fabrication.
- Extra events, event production, dedicated staffing, and bespoke content.
- Demo tables, recurring office hours, product sampling, custom research, and enhanced reporting.
- Merchandise procurement, storage, replenishment, and gifting beyond a tightly defined allowance.

The counterargument to a core-plus-add-ons model is that it may look less generous than an all-inclusive founding package. The reason for the boundary is that custom work otherwise becomes an unpriced agency service and makes renewal delivery progressively harder.

### 4. Twenty sponsors require an attention-allocation system

The café cannot give 20 brands equal high-attention presence every day. That would make the house feel like a trade-show floor and damage the community asset sponsors want to access.

Use four distinct layers:

1. **Always-on recognition:** low-visual-weight acknowledgement for all current partners.
2. **Rotating features:** one or a tightly capped number of sponsors featured at a time, with each partner’s annual allocation stated numerically. Twenty two-week features would occupy 40 weeks, leaving 12 weeks for house or community programming; this is capacity math to test, not a final calendar.
3. **Quarterly sponsor-led events:** up to 80 event uses annually at full inventory, alongside community programming and normal trade.
4. **Optional activations:** booked, staff-intensive formats with separate limits and pricing.

“Daily activation” should mean that something useful can happen in the house on an ordinary day, not that every sponsor is activated every day.

Potential formats worth testing include:

- An **opportunity menu** containing sponsor-supported credits, grants, jobs, beta access, learning sessions, and office hours alongside café offerings.
- Sponsor-specific QR offers with a clear visitor benefit and a trackable next step.
- Merchandise unlocked by voluntary participation or an expressed interest, not covert profiling or unconditional distribution volume.
- Opt-in product trials, demos, research conversations, and introductions in defined zones and hours.
- Sponsor-free areas or periods, no unsolicited pitching, and house approval over content and category suitability.

### 5. Quarterly events and the food-and-beverage benefit need exact terms

At full inventory, quarterly use means up to 80 sponsor events annually, roughly 1.5 per week before house programming. Sponsors curating and running their own events reduces production work but does not remove scheduling, approvals, staffing, setup, safety, cleaning, or reputational exposure.

Before sale, define:

- Number of included uses and whether unused quarters expire.
- Event duration, setup and teardown time, capacity, eligible days, blackout dates, and booking windows.
- Included room, furniture, AV, staffing, security, cleaning, and service charges.
- Sponsor responsibilities, guest curation, content approval, cancellation, and damage terms.
- Whether venue hire is included and whether production costs extra.
- The 20% food-and-beverage discount’s eligible menu, minimum spend, guest cap, exclusions, taxes, and service charges.

Model the annual calendar and sample event margins before publishing the benefit. Prime-date demand can exceed capacity even when the annual total appears feasible.

### 6. Membership and sponsorship now form two recurring revenue engines

The updated model is no longer sponsorship alone. Founder membership costs ₹2 lakh for the first membership year and ₹1 lakh annually thereafter. Sponsorship remains ₹15 lakh per year for up to 20 category-exclusive partners. Café purchases and event transactions may add revenue, but their margins are not documented.

This reduces dependence on brands but creates a second high-price product to deliver and renew. There is no documented venue-specific member willingness to pay, sponsor sell-through rate, payment timing, activation cost, operating break-even, or renewal history. Annual sponsorship and founding-member pre-sales also create a timing mismatch: construction cash is needed before the year of benefits can begin.

A full financial model needs separate treatment of:

- Build deposits, construction, equipment, licences, taxes, pre-opening payroll, contingency, and working capital.
- Recurring café, member-programming, and event operations.
- New-member sales, onboarding, hosting, introductions, room access, programming, and renewal costs.
- Sponsor sales commissions, onboarding, activation delivery, event support, reporting, discounts, and merchandise handling.
- Partial member and sponsor sell-through, delayed opening, cancellations, non-renewals, refunds, and replacement sales.
- Contract commencement, payment milestones, delay remedies, refunds or credits, benefit substitution, and termination.

Model member and sponsor scenarios independently. Do not add full-sellout sponsorship to an assumed member total and call the sum a forecast. Capacity, first-year price treatment, renewal eligibility, and actual cash collection dates must reconcile.

The recommended validation sequence is a paid founding-member cohort and a small paid founding-partner pilot through confirmed community activity, followed by bounded annual offers and appropriately conditioned pre-sales. Keep the membership price, ₹15 lakh sponsor price, member capacity, and 20-slot sponsor ceiling as hypotheses to test, not assumptions to defend.

### 7. Measurement must stop at the evidence

QR scans, redemptions, trials, introductions, and sales are different events. A scan is not a lead; a lead is not a conversion; a redemption is not incremental revenue.

A useful reporting chain is:

1. Verified event attendance or café transactions, using documented counting rules.
2. Activation participation and tracked campaign-page visits.
3. Verified offer redemptions.
4. Explicit opt-in enquiries or introductions.
5. Sponsor-side product use or sales attribution, only where the sponsor supplies it.

Reports should define sources, deduplication, refunds, offline handling, reporting delays, corrections, and data cutoffs. Individual contact details require separate, explicit consent describing purpose and recipient. Do not hand over directory, attendee, or newsletter lists. Default to aggregate reporting and document retention, access, withdrawal, and deletion responsibilities.

### 8. The naming and brand hierarchy remain unresolved

Use this hierarchy until naming is cleared:

- **Fourthspace:** working title for the proposed international masterbrand; not cleared.
- **Fourthspace HSR:** proposed first physical café and event space; not an operating global location in a network.
- **HSR Founders Club:** originating community and source of historical evidence.
- **Katte:** founding cultural inspiration and operating principle, not a second simultaneous masterbrand.
- **Global expansion:** long-term vision only.

“Fourthspace” is already used by a [London architecture practice](https://www.fourthspace.co.uk/) and “The Fourth Space” by a [Canadian community organisation](https://www.thefourthspace.ca/index.html). “Fourth place” also has several existing definitions, including [meaning-making](https://thesfcommons.substack.com/p/introducing-the-fourth-place-and) and digital community. A worldwide trademark and domain screen is required before external brand investment.

The name scales cleanly by city, but the concept cannot depend on readers already knowing “third place” theory. Lead with a plain descriptor and use “where people meet possibility” only as a secondary line. Keep “LinkedIn as a house” internal; it suggests profiles, search, recruiting, identity systems, and a possible LinkedIn affiliation that are not part of the offer.

Comparable naming patterns support this caution. Global venues frequently use House/Home/Commons language, while Bengaluru’s more distinctive places often use local words and social rituals, as shown in the [Bengaluru third-place directory](https://ooru.space/cities/bengaluru/). Katte itself is already used by [KaTTe – The Creative Community](https://ooru.space/spaces/katte-the-creative-community/) in Indiranagar.

Future locations should express shared hospitality and sponsorship principles through locally authored programming. A second city should follow evidence of repeat visitation, contribution margin, reliable delivery, and sponsor renewal at HSR—not precede it.

### 9. The collateral must support an internal purchase decision

The prospectus should be understandable without a verbal pitch and should contain:

1. **Proposal at a glance:** proposed HSR location, annual partnership, ₹15 lakh price, document date, and status.
2. **Concrete place description:** café, event space, intended audience, access model, and what an ordinary visit involves.
3. **Origin:** HSR Founders Club story and the specific Katte principles carried into the house.
4. **Community evidence:** attributed, dated, defined figures and representative event examples.
5. **Sponsor objective and audience fit:** tailored to the recipient rather than a generic list of brands.
6. **Core benefit matrix:** quantity, frequency, placement, responsibilities, included costs, exclusions, and measurement for every item.
7. **Attention and event calendar:** shared recognition, rotations, quarterly sponsor-led events, and visitor safeguards.
8. **Optional services:** separately scoped production and activation work.
9. **Measurement and consent plan:** reportable signals and their limitations, without fabricated forecasts.
10. **Terms and next step:** category definition, term commencement, payment and tax treatment, delay/cancellation handling, validity date, named contact, and a category-fit conversation.

Include a one-page forwardable summary. Keep ₹3 crore seller arithmetic, global expansion, and speculative footfall out of the buyer’s lead story. Label conceptual renders and mockups as artist’s impressions, never operating proof.

### 10. Existing project material can be reused selectively

- `output/pdf/katte-sponsorship/build_deck.py` and `sponsor.json` already provide a ten-page sponsor-specific PDF structure, proposed OpenAI drink concept, annual ₹15 lakh framing, measurement boundaries, and cup/menu mockups. Reuse the document mechanics and careful status labels; do not reuse OpenAI as an implied partner or assume the single-drink package is the right new offer.
- `docs/house-understanding/house-understanding.html`, `PHOTO-LABELS.md`, and the house plans can make the venue tangible. Use high-confidence photographs and label proposed-use renders. Spatial documentation does not establish legal occupancy or event capacity.
- `PRODUCT.md`, `site/index.html`, and the public “Space” identity conflict with the Networker Space, Katte, and Fourthspace naming stages. The site also contains present-tense operating language while this brief treats the venue as proposed. Resolve these status and identity conflicts before directing sponsors to the site.
- No verified sponsor logos, testimonials, case studies, activation outcomes, category commitments, or venue-performance metrics were found.

### 11. Comparator: The Commons, San Francisco

All six public internal pages were reviewed on 2026-10-26: [Home](https://www.thesfcommons.com/), [About](https://www.thesfcommons.com/about), [Events](https://www.thesfcommons.com/events), [Membership](https://www.thesfcommons.com/membership), [Rentals](https://www.thesfcommons.com/rentals), and [Host at the Commons](https://www.thesfcommons.com/host-at-the-commons). The linked [day-pass page](https://the-commons-guests.vercel.app/day-pass), [fourth-place essay](https://thesfcommons.substack.com/p/introducing-the-fourth-place-and), and [18-page rental brochure](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1MxeEb7Ds6rNP_P2UA_u007LJpsOZMXfK/view) were also reviewed.

**Their fourth-place definition is already specific—and differs from ours.** The Commons defines a fourth place as a physical setting for meaning-making through intentional programming and pluralistic discussion, consistent encounters, and a supportive community. Its essay distinguishes “space” as objective geography from “place” as geography with emotional attachment and shared memory. Fourthspace would therefore borrow a category term whose best-known proponent deliberately chose “place,” while giving it a different professional-opportunity meaning. This increases naming and comprehension risk.

**Their philosophy directly challenges transactional networking—and Fourthspace now deliberately differs.** The fourth-place essay criticizes vocational clubs where favors are transacted and status is displayed. Testimonials repeatedly emphasize belonging, not having to perform an identity, friendship, curiosity, and personal transformation. Fourthspace will not adopt The Commons’ meaning-making definition. Its category is the **professional-opportunity house**, continuous with HSR Founders Club’s story: repeated, trusted encounters help people discover professional relationships and opportunities. Opportunity is the explicit value; trust and hospitality are how the house protects that value from becoming transactional extraction. The Commons is an operating reference, not the philosophical model.

**The operating engine is member agency, not venue inventory.** Members can host small events, coordinate spontaneous meetings through Slack, experiment with facilitation, and turn interests into recurring clubs. Programming spans Discover, Expand, Heal, and Actualize across mind, body, heart, and soul. The broader lesson is that repeated member-to-member contribution creates the community; the house does not have to produce every interaction. For Fourthspace, members should be able to create opportunities for one another through breakfasts, clinics, founder circles, demos, skill exchanges, and introductions without a sponsor attached to each one.

**The Commons uses several revenue and access mechanisms.** Its Home page lists membership at **$190 month-to-month or $150 per month when paid quarterly**, with scholarships for financial hardship. It calls memberships the primary financial support and says this helps it avoid VC funding and values-misaligned corporate rentals. The [day-pass page](https://the-commons-guests.vercel.app/day-pass) lists **$30 per day**, while Sundays and public events are free. These are The Commons’ published prices, not benchmarks for Fourthspace’s economics.

The [rental brochure](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1MxeEb7Ds6rNP_P2UA_u007LJpsOZMXfK/view) lists the 550 Studio at **$450/hour standard and $350/hour for nonprofits**, discounted member rates, and **no rental fee plus 40% of tickets for public ticketed events** (page 17). It specifies a 100-person main-room capacity, smaller add-on rooms, equipment, setup and teardown allowances, staff support, event formats, and four named case studies. This is the level of operational clarity Fourthspace’s event benefit needs.

**Gatherers separates recurring community hosting from private rental.** The Gatherers program combines ordinary membership, applications for monthly or biweekly hosting without standard rental rates, access to the existing community, calendar and member-channel promotion, and a peer circle of hosts. Paid events may involve commission or revenue share; the reviewed page does not publish a Gatherer fee. Purely transactional events, hard-sell workshops, private parties, and operationally heavy formats are explicitly poor fits. This is a useful model for a future Fourthspace host program, but it should not be copied as a sponsor tier without separating community hosts from paying brands.

**Their model informs but does not determine Fourthspace’s revenue architecture.** The Commons protects its culture through member funding and selective rentals. Fourthspace now also has founder membership as a recurring revenue stream alongside sponsorship, but it is restricted to founders and centered on professional opportunity rather than general personal exploration. Community independence still needs explicit protection: no member-list access, no unsolicited sales, sponsor-free member programming, relevance review, and the right to reject hard-sell activations. Venue hire can remain a separate transactional option rather than becoming another membership tier.

**Their public evidence also shows why content governance matters.** The Membership page says “800+” member-only events while Home says “thousands.” Pages variously show 9am and 10am opening times, and one FAQ says the space is member-only seven days a week while other pages describe public Sundays. These are site inconsistencies, not grounds to dismiss their model. Fourthspace should avoid the same problem by giving membership, hours, event counts, pricing, and availability one owner and one current source of truth.

The practical transfer to Fourthspace is therefore:

- Make repeated encounters and member contribution the community engine.
- Define safety as freedom from performance pressure and unwanted solicitation, not only physical security.
- Separate public access, founder membership, community hosting, private rental, and brand partnership.
- Publish exact event capacities, inclusions, operating responsibilities, and prices when confirmed.
- Use real event case studies and current evidence instead of abstract claims about possibility.
- Position Fourthspace explicitly as a professional-opportunity house; do not adopt The Commons’ meaning-making definition.

### 12. Integrated founder-membership, sponsorship, and house model

The house should have **three distinct ways in, not three membership tiers**:

1. **Public café guest:** anyone can walk in, buy food and drink, attend selected public programming, and discover the community. A walk-in is not a member and must not be assumed to be a founder.
2. **Founder member:** a vetted founder pays ₹2 lakh for the first membership year and ₹1 lakh annually thereafter. This is the only membership; the earlier Silver, Gold, Platinum, and lifetime-tier direction should be retired or explicitly treated as obsolete.
3. **Brand partner:** a company buys a defined annual sponsorship. It does not automatically become a member, receive founder data, or gain unrestricted access to member programming. Private event hire, if offered, is a separate transaction rather than a fourth membership.

#### Founder membership is the relationship product

The ₹2 lakh initial price cannot be justified by desks, café discounts, or room access alone. The core product is trusted access to other founders and a repeated system for making useful professional encounters happen.

Recommended components for validation are:

- Founder-only eligibility with a published definition and review process. Early-team employees, operators, investors, and service providers can be welcomed in the public café or invited to specific programs without being called founder members.
- An opt-in founder profile covering what the person is building, what they can help with, and what they currently need. Sponsors do not receive this profile database.
- A lightweight **ask/offer system** for hiring, pilots, customer discovery, technical help, cofounder searches, fundraising preparation, and other founder needs. The house facilitates; it does not guarantee outcomes.
- Thoughtful introductions with consent from both sides, rather than automated lead distribution.
- Founder circles, breakfasts, demo sessions, product clinics, skill exchanges, and member-hosted formats. Not every event should have a sponsor.
- Bookable quiet and meeting rooms, founder-only areas, and clear guest and team-access rules.
- Hosting rights that let members create value for one another, subject to curation and capacity.
- A digital continuation of the physical relationships using simple existing tools before any custom app is considered.

Membership renewal depends on whether founders continue to receive useful relationships, recognition, support, and opportunities. Measure repeat participation, accepted introductions, member-hosted activity, and voluntary reports of outcomes. Do not advertise funding, jobs, customers, or cofounders as guaranteed membership results.

#### Sponsorship is the business-support product

Sponsors fund useful infrastructure, programming, and offers for the same community, but they do not own it. Their opportunity is relevant engagement with public café guests and consenting founders, not access to a captive audience. Founder-only membership and category-exclusive sponsorship must remain separate contracts, audiences, and reporting systems.

A sponsor can support an opportunity menu, founder credits, product clinics, a relevant merchandise shelf, or a quarterly sponsor-led event. Members must be free to ignore these without losing membership value. No sponsor should be able to buy founder status, member-list access, unsolicited introductions, or control of house curation.

#### The documented house supports zoning by audience and time

The physical source is a converted four-level house, not an empty office floor. `docs/house-understanding/house-understanding.html` and `PHOTO-LABELS.md` record two double-height spaces, separate basement entrances, a first-floor outside entrance, two kitchens, multiple living/dining rooms, and a terrace. The following uses are **proposed**, not confirmed room allocations or capacities:

- **Ground floor — public face and café.** The existing arrival court, foyer, teak-floored Living 1, dining/family rooms, galley kitchen, bedrooms, and front lawn provide the clearest public café setting. Use it for walk-ins, hospitality, low-key sponsor recognition, the opportunity menu, and a bounded merchandise display. The garden connection supports lingering rather than a transactional media wall.
- **Lower level — focused founder work and making.** The documented office suite, Court 1 with the big tree, and workroom/gallery can support founder work, small workshops, maker activity, and a founder lounge. The separate office-side entrance may help manage member access independently of the café, subject to on-site verification.
- **First floor — meetings, learning, and events.** The olive-tree courtyard, Living 2, Dining 2, Family 2, library, kitchen, and Bedroom 3 can support bookable meetings, founder circles, shared meals, and the main evening event room. Its outside stair can help separate event arrivals from public café flow. Quarterly sponsor-led events belong here within an agreed calendar, not across every floor.
- **Second floor — quiet founder rooms and small hosted gatherings.** The two existing bedrooms, study nook, dressing room, and long terrace can support closed-door work and small founder dinners or conversations. Do not promise large rooftop parties, grilling, occupancy, or late-night use until safety, noise, neighbours, and permitted use are checked.

The house’s separate entrances and floor changes are useful only if access control and operations preserve the distinctions. Legal occupancy, accessibility, fire safety, event capacities, kitchen use, noise limits, and neighbourhood impact remain unverified.

#### A simple daily rhythm connects the products

- **08:00–18:00:** public café on the ground floor; founder work and making below and above; bookable meetings on the first floor. Sponsor presence stays bounded and useful.
- **18:00–23:00:** work stops and the house shifts to founder programming, selected public events, private bookings, and scheduled sponsor-led events. The first floor is the primary event zone; the terrace is for appropriately sized gatherings only.
- **Between visits:** members can continue asks, offers, introductions, and hosting through a lightweight community system. Sponsors receive only agreed campaign reporting and explicit opt-ins.

This preserves the existing café-by-day/club-by-night idea while making the founder-only boundary clear. Public visitors provide discovery and café activity; founder members create recurring relationship value; sponsors help finance useful experiences.

#### Revenue must be modeled from actual counts and timing

Use separate formulas rather than a combined headline:

- First-year founder memberships: number of new paid founders × ₹2 lakh, including that year’s membership.
- Membership renewals: number of eligible renewing founders × ₹1 lakh annually.
- Sponsorship: number of signed category partners × ₹15 lakh annually, up to the proposed 20.
- Café, private event hire, ticketed events, and add-ons: separate volume, price, direct-cost, and margin models.

Member capacity must follow room capacity, hosting quality, and programming load—not the revenue target. Founder lifetime value and sponsor contracted value must not be presented as cash available for construction.

#### Existing public material now needs reconciliation

`PRODUCT.md` and `site/index.html` currently describe three ways in, Silver/Gold/Platinum or member/lifetime distinctions, 20 lifetime places, and eligibility for founders, early teams, operators, and people around startups. The updated model instead has public café access, one founder-only membership, and up to 20 sponsor categories. The site’s “price to come,” operating-present-tense, guest, team, lifetime, and evening-access copy must be revised before it can support the new collateral. Do not confuse 20 sponsor slots with any legacy 20-member concept.

## Goals vs. Non-Goals

### In-Scope Goals

- Define a credible first-house founder-membership and sponsorship proposition for HSR Layout.
- Preserve HSR Founders Club as the origin and proof of relevant community-building experience.
- Build one founder-only membership around trusted relationships and professional opportunities.
- Test the ₹2 lakh initial membership price, ₹1 lakh annual renewal, ₹15 lakh annual sponsor price, and up-to-20 category-exclusive structure.
- Zone the documented four-level house for public café guests, founder work, meetings, events, and small hosted gatherings.
- Design useful daily engagement, rotating sponsor attention, and quarterly sponsor-led event access.
- Protect visitor and member trust through relevance, consent, activation limits, and honest reporting.
- Produce a forwardable standalone sponsor prospectus and clear cup, menu, shelf, and event-space visualizations after the offer is bounded.
- Establish principles that could support future cities without claiming an existing network.

### Out-of-Scope Non-Goals

- Presenting ₹3 crore as secured revenue or construction funding.
- Treating HSR Founders Club attendance, directory entries, or newsletter signups as Fourthspace footfall or sponsor-consenting leads.
- Guaranteeing impressions, leads, product adoption, sales, ROI, sponsor renewals, funding, jobs, customers, cofounders, or other membership outcomes.
- Calling non-founders founder members or automatically granting sponsors membership, founder data, or unrestricted community access.
- Retaining the obsolete Silver/Gold/Platinum structure or conflating legacy lifetime-member places with sponsor slots.
- Building a custom community app before the membership workflow is validated with simple tools.
- Giving all 20 sponsors equal high-attention activation every day.
- Monthly Fourthspace-produced events for every sponsor.
- Sharing community contact lists or personal data without separate permission.
- Selling multi-city rights, global exclusivity, or access to unopened locations.
- Finalizing the Fourthspace name, logo, domains, or signage before clearance.
- Producing a large custom activation program without separately defined staffing and cost.

## Key Decisions & Staging for Next Steps

### Decisions established by the working brief

- The first deliverable is standalone sponsor collateral.
- Fourthspace explicitly owns professional opportunity, continuous with HSR Founders Club; it does not adopt The Commons’ meaning-making definition.
- Founder membership and sponsorship are the two stated recurring revenue engines; sponsorship can help fund the build without being treated as the only revenue source.
- Membership is for founders only: ₹2 lakh includes the first membership year, and each subsequent annual renewal costs ₹1 lakh.
- The earlier Silver/Gold/Platinum and lifetime-tier direction is superseded by public café access, one founder membership, and brand partnerships.
- The starting sponsorship hypothesis is ₹15 lakh per year, up to 20 category-exclusive partners.
- Quarterly event-space access replaces the earlier monthly-hosted-event interpretation.
- Sponsors curate and run their events; the house provides the space and the proposed 20% food-and-beverage benefit.
- Daily walk-in engagement and relevant merchandising should provide recurring value between events.
- Katte is the founding cultural idea; global expansion is a stated ambition.
- The HSR Founders Club origin must appear prominently in the narrative.

### Recommended decisions requiring owner approval

- Use Fourthspace only as a working title until international clearance is complete.
- Keep “LinkedIn as a house” internal and lead externally with the concrete professional-opportunity-house description.
- Validate both products through a paid founding-member cohort and a small paid sponsor pilot before treating either as dependable financing.
- Set founder eligibility, membership capacity, guest rules, hosting rights, renewal conditions, and opportunity-reporting boundaries.
- Use the proposed floor zoning only after on-site capacity, safety, access, and permitted-use checks.
- Separate the annual sponsor core package from custom, staff-intensive add-ons.
- Use shared recognition plus capped rotations instead of permanent high-attention placement for every sponsor.
- Preserve a house experience that can succeed for visitors and founder members even when they ignore every sponsor.

### Gates before external sponsor circulation

1. **Evidence:** substantiate the HSR Founders Club figures with dates, sources, and definitions; confirm permission for publication.
2. **Name and status:** clear Fourthspace or choose another name; align the site and collateral on proposed versus operating status.
3. **Demand:** interview economic buyers and test one concrete sponsor objective, package, and price through a paid pilot or conditioned pre-sale.
4. **Financial viability:** complete build, operating, activation, event-margin, payment-timing, and downside models.
5. **Physical capacity:** verify venue control, occupancy, permitted uses, accessibility, safety, storage, activation zones, and annual event capacity.
6. **Commercial terms:** define category rights, core benefits, event and discount rules, term commencement, payment, taxes, delays, cancellations, and make-goods.
7. **Measurement and consent:** pilot the tracking chain and approve privacy, consent, retention, and sponsor-reporting practices.
8. **Fulfillment:** assign owners for sales, booking, approvals, merchandise, activations, reporting, invoicing, renewals, and incidents.

### Suggested staging

1. Verify evidence, naming, venue constraints, founder eligibility, and member and sponsor demand.
2. Test the founder ask/offer, introduction, hosting, and programming workflow with a paid founding-member cohort.
3. Run one bounded paid community sponsorship and close-out report.
4. Finalize membership terms, floor zoning, annual sponsor core/add-on packages, and commercial schedules.
